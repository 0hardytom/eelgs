Project Updates

Complete

•HST photometry extraction pipeline for identified sources: The pipeline now automatically queries the HST legacy archive for imaging in source fields, and uses photutils to perform aperture photometry on our candidate EELGs. This has been tested on our s780 and MACS0159 fields and is able to reliably extract photometry for sources with MUSE detections. This will allow for SED fitting to be performed on our candidates to extract stellar masses.
•SED fitting implementation for HST pipeline: I have now implemented a wrapper for the bagpipes SED fitting code which takes the output from our HST photometry pipeline and uses this to produce stellar mass estimates for our sources. At the moment this is using a relatively simple star formation history but it is able to produce reliable fits for our test sources and the mass estimates are consistent with what we would expect from the literature.
•Mass-Metallicity-SFR plot for MUSE-WIDE: Using the public MUSE-WIDE survey data I have extracted the mass, metallicity and SFR for their identified sources to produce a comparison sample for our candidates. Our sources appear to lie on the same fundamental plane as the MUSE-WIDE sources, although our sample is biased towards higher SFRs as expected.
•Population investigations - LogN/LogS for candidate types, redshift distributions and survey comparisons: I have now produced LogN/LogS plots for our different candidate types (starburst, AGN, composite) and compared these to the expectations from the MUSE-WIDE survey. Our number counts are broadly consistent with the literature, although we see a slight excess of AGN candidates at faint fluxes which may be due to contamination from star-forming galaxies.
•Re-implementation of Jiang19+ metallicity methods: After the issues with our O32 ratios I have gone back and re-implemented the metallicity diagnostics from Jiang+19 directly from their paper. This has resolved the discrepancy and our O32 ratios are now much more consistent with the literature values. This has also improved our R23 estimates and the distribution now much more closely resembles the Jiang+19 spread of green pea galaxies.
•Investigated Metallicity as an AGN tracer: I have investigated using the mass-metallicity relation as a potential AGN tracer. The idea is that AGN hosts should be more massive and metal-rich than star-forming galaxies at the same redshift. While there is a general trend in this direction, there is significant overlap between the two populations so this is not a reliable standalone tracer, but could be used in combination with other methods.

To Complete Over Christmas

•Narrowing down green-pea selection criteria to match literature: Now that the metallicity and line ratio issues have been resolved, the next step is to apply a strict set of selection criteria to our sample to identify a robust list of green pea candidates. This will involve making cuts on line ratios, equivalent widths, and stellar mass to match the criteria used in the literature.
•Implementing HST recovery and SED fitting for most extreme candidates: I will apply the new HST photometry and SED fitting pipeline to our most extreme candidates (ie those with the highest O32/R23 ratios) to obtain robust stellar mass estimates for them. This will be crucial for understanding their location on the mass-metallicity relation.
•Implementing Mass-Metallicity for extreme candidates: Once the stellar masses have been extracted I will place our extreme candidates on the mass-metallicity relation to see if they lie in the same region as other green pea galaxies from the literature. This will be a key test of whether our selection criteria are successfully identifying the correct population of galaxies.
•Further investigation of AGN tracers: While the BPT diagram is a good first-order AGN tracer, it is not perfect and can be contaminated by star-forming galaxies with high ionisation parameters. I will investigate other AGN tracers such as radio and X-ray emission by cross-matching our sample with catalogues from VLA and Chandra.
•Write-up: I will begin to write up the methods section of the first paper, detailing the data reduction, source identification and analysis pipeline that has been developed over the last few months. This will form the basis of the first project paper.
