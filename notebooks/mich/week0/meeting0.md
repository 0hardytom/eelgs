## Main Objective

The primary goal of this project is to leverage the large, shallow Multi Unit Spectroscopic Explorer (MUSE) survey data to identify and characterize a complete sample of emission-line galaxies. This work aims to fill a "blind spot" in current large-scale surveys, particularly the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) survey, which provides excellent galaxy selection based on the [OII] emission line but lacks information on other critical diagnostic lines (e.g., [OIII], Hα, Hβ).

The deadline for extracting the complete sample from the MUSE data cubes is the **end of March**.

---

## Key Research Questions

1.  **Sample Properties:** What are the fundamental properties of the emission-line galaxies we identify? This includes measuring their emissivity, stellar masses, infrared (IR) luminosities, and the equivalent widths of their emission lines relative to the stellar continuum.

2.  **Sample Completeness & Selection Biases:**
    -   What are the selection effects in our sample? We need to quantify the completeness.
    -   Which types of galaxies are we likely missing and why?
    -   How does the brightness of specific emission lines (e.g., [OIII]) relative to atmospheric skylines affect our detection efficiency? At certain redshifts, key lines can fall within bright skylines, rendering them invisible.
    -   What is the nature of the optical counterparts to these galaxies?

3.  **Influence of Gravitational Lensing:**
    -   How many of the detected galaxies are visible because they are located within massive galaxy clusters (intracluster members)?
    -   How many are background galaxies whose light is being gravitationally amplified (lensed) by a foreground cluster?
    -   Are there any instances of multiply-lensed galaxies? (Expectation is that these will be rare).

## Methodology & Action Items

1.  **Generate a Gold-Standard Sample:**
    -   Identify a robust sample of approximately 20-30 good examples of emission-line galaxies from the MUSE data cubes.
    -   This initial sample will serve as a baseline for understanding the broader population.

2.  **Compare with Deep Field Data:**
    -   Our survey consists of ~200 shallow fields, which contrasts with the ~10-15 existing deep MUSE fields (e.g., the Hubble Ultra-Deep Field).
    -   Use the results from these deep observations as a baseline to understand what we *should* expect to find in terms of galaxy populations and number densities. This will help in quantifying the completeness of our shallow survey.

3.  **Quantify Completeness:**
    -   Develop a method to rigorously quantify the completeness of the galaxy sample. This must account for biases such as:
        -   **Skyline Contamination:** Identify redshift ranges where key lines like [OIII] are obscured by bright atmospheric emission lines.
        -   **Surface Brightness Limits:** Our shallow data will have a limit on the faintest objects we can detect.
        -   **Lensing Magnification:** Account for the bias introduced by gravitational lensing from foreground clusters.
