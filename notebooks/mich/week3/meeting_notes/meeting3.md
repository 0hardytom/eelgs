## Primary Goals & Methodology

The main objective is to establish a robust method for analyzing the spectral properties of the brightest sources in the MUSE data.

1.  **Dispersion and Brightness Ratios:**
    -   The immediate task is to select the brightest emission line source and analyze its velocity dispersion.
    -   A key test of our data quality and systematic uncertainties will be to assess the repeatability of the brightness ratios between different emission lines at the same velocity.

2.  **Velocity Measurement:**
    -   The line profiles used for velocity measurements are expected to be consistent (i.e., of the same order). However, it is crucial to remain aware of the instrumental resolution and its potential impact on the derived kinematics.

---

## Gravitational Lensing & Background Flux

-   The use of **gravitational lensing tools** is a future consideration. This will become relevant later in the project.
-   There appears to be an interesting geometric effect related to lensing. While our observations are amplifying the target sources, this effect might also be "zooming in" on the background, potentially complicating the analysis.

## Next Steps & Action Items

1.  **Cross-instrument Calibration (HST & MUSE):**
    -   **HST Photometry:** The first step is to characterize the continuum brightness of our sources using archival HST data.
    -   **Continuum Agreement:** A critical check is to ensure that the continuum level measured by MUSE is consistent with the HST photometry.
    -   **Quantify Line vs. Continuum Flux:** If the instruments are in agreement, the next step is to quantify the relative contribution of the emission lines versus the stellar continuum to the total observed light.
    -   **Magnitude Calibration:** We need to establish a combined HST/MUSE magnitude calibration. This will involve using bright, unsaturated objects in the field to ensure our flux measurements are accurate.

2.  **Spectral Line Analysis:**
    -   **Sky Lines:** A thorough check of the sky lines is necessary to ensure proper subtraction and to identify any potential residuals that could contaminate our spectra.
    -   **Neon & Hydrogen Lines:** We need to compare the lines from Neon II (NeII) and Hydrogen (HS) to check for consistency.
    -   **Balmer Series:** The co-added spectrum should be used to verify that the Balmer series lines are consistent with theoretical expectations.

3.  **Specific Analysis Tasks:**
    -   **Balmer Continuum:** Create a plot specifically designed to identify and measure the Balmer continuum. A key question is to determine the contribution of the nebular continuum relative to the stellar continuum.
    -   **Background Mapping:** Generate a background map for the area near our primary source to better understand the local environment.
    -   **Balmer Line Plot:** Produce a plot showing purely the Balmer emission lines. This plot should also be used to investigate the presence of a Balmer break in the spectrum. Co-adding the data may help to improve the signal-to-noise for this feature.
    -   **3D Spectrum Visualization:** For detailed analysis, the plan is to select a specific region of interest and generate a 3D plot of the spectrum.

## Key Observations & Notes

-   A noticeable "bump" in the continuum has been observed, which is suspected to be a contribution from the intracluster light or unresolved cluster galaxies.
